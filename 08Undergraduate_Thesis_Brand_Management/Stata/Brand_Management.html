Brand Management and Consumer Perception

Dynamic Stata Report

Study: Undergraduate thesis (2022)
Empirical setting: International apparel industry
Estimation method: Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)

About this replication

This dynamic document preserves scripts and outputs of the six Stata regression models used in the undergraduate thesis The Impact of Brand Management on Consumer Perception: Evidence from the International Apparel Industry.

Conventions

  • Each reported regression uses 31 complete observations.
  • The models are OLS regressions with an intercept.
  • Statistical significance is discussed at the 5% level unless otherwise stated.
  • Coefficients are interpreted as associations conditional on the variables in each specification; the models do not by themselves establish causal effects.
  • Source datasets are loaded but never overwritten by this report.

1. Overall Brand Management and Total Consumer Perception

1.1 Research question

The first model examines whether the aggregate Brand Management measure is associated with Total Consumer Perception.

The specification is:

Total Consumer Perception = β₀ + β₁(Brand Management) + ε

2. Brand Management Dimensions and Total Consumer Perception

2.1 Research question

The second model decomposes Brand Management into four dimensions:

  1. Brand Positioning
  2. Brand Image
  3. Brand Extension
  4. Brand Marketing

The model evaluates their conditional associations with Total Consumer Perception:

Total Consumer Perception = β₀ + β₁(Positioning) + β₂(Image) + β₃(Extension) + β₄(Marketing) + ε

3. Consumer Perception Components

  • ED Expansion Driver for the Brand Awareness analysis;
  • LD Loyalty Driver for the Brand Attitude/Loyalty analysis; and
  • PD Price Driver for the Purchase Intention analysis.

5. Cross-Model Synthesis

The models reveal a differentiated pattern across the Brand Management dimensions:

Model Outcome Statistically significant predictors (5% level)
3.1 Total Consumer Perception Overall Brand Management (+)
3.2 Total Consumer Perception Brand Positioning (+); Brand Marketing (+)
3.3.1 Brand Awareness / ED Brand Positioning (+); Brand Marketing (+)
3.3.2 Brand Attitude / LD Brand Image (+)
3.3.3 Purchase Intention / PD Brand Positioning (+); Brand Image (+); Brand Marketing (−)
3.4 Brand Value Brand Positioning (+); Brand Marketing (+)

Across these specifications, Brand Positioning and Brand Marketing appear repeatedly in statistically significant relationships, although the surviving Purchase Intention model produces a negative Brand Marketing coefficient. Brand Image is particularly relevant in the Brand Attitude/Loyalty and Purchase Intention models. Brand Extension is not statistically significant at the 5% level in the multiple-regression specifications.

These findings describe the associations in the thesis’s analytical design and sample. They should not be interpreted as establishing causal effects.
